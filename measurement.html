Sky Coordinates (RA & DEC)
The celestial equivalent of longitude and latitude. Right Ascension (RA) measures how far east an object is along the celestial equator, while Declination (DEC) measures how far north or south it sits in the sky.
102 Infrared Spectral Bands
Unlike standard cameras that capture only 3 colors (RGB), SPHEREx splits invisible infrared light into 102 distinct wavelengths. Each band acts like a cosmic barcode, revealing the chemical makeup, temperature, and distance of stars and galaxies.
Caltech/IPAC IRSA Level 2 Pipeline
NASA's automated processing system that converts raw telescope sensor signals into calibrated, science-ready sky exposures—complete with precise RA/DEC coordinates and true infrared brightness measurements.
1. TAP Table: spherex.artifact
IRSA Catalog Index (spherex.artifact)
Queries NASA’s Table Access Protocol (TAP) database to locate and fetch every calibrated SPHEREx Level 2 exposure overlapping your target’s sky coordinates.
2. WCS Distortion: North-Up TAN (6.2"/px)
Sky Alignment & Scale (North-Up TAN • 6.2"/px)
Reprojects raw, rotated telescope frames onto a flat tangent-plane grid with Celestial North pointing straight up, preserving SPHEREx’s native 6.2 arcseconds-per-pixel resolution.
3. ZODI Model: Subtracted (HDU 4)
Zodiacal Glow Removal (ZODI Subtracted • HDU 4)
Subtracts foreground infrared sunlight scattered by Solar System dust (stored in FITS layer HDU 4), leaving a clean dark background so faint stars and moving objects stand out clearly.
Gaia DR3 Star Catalog
The European Space Agency’s landmark 3D map of 1.8 billion stars. Provides ultra-precise sky coordinates (RA/DEC), distances, and proper motion vectors used to track how stars move across time.
1. Covered Channels (17 / 102)
Covered Spectral Channels
Number of SPHEREx’s 102 infrared wavelength bands that have valid, unmasked observations at this exact sky coordinate.
2. Peak Flux (280.5 μJy)
Peak Infrared Flux (μJy)
The highest measured brightness across all covered wavelength bands, recorded in microjanskys (μJy) to show where the object emits the strongest infrared energy.
3. Mean SNR (5.1)
Mean Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR)
Measures signal clarity relative to background noise across all extracted bands. Values of 5.0 or higher confirm a statistically reliable detection.
4. Aperture Radius (6.2 arcsec)
Photometric Aperture Radius (6.2")
Radius of the circular measurement zone centered on the target—matching SPHEREx’s native 1-pixel scale (6.2 arcsec) to isolate its light from nearby stars.
